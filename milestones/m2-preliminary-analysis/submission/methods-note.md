# Methods

We analyzed the Public Health Agency of Canada's public file (`SubstanceHarmsData.csv`), selecting annual opioid-death crude rates (overall and by sex) for 2016-2025. Of 33,250 source records, 420 rate records formed 140 region-years across 14 regions (13 provinces and territories plus Canada's national series). Before exclusions, the overall rate was missing for 6 region-years (4 suppressed, 2 unavailable) and each sex-specific rate for 25 (23 suppressed, 2 unavailable). The male-to-female ratio, calculated only when both sex-specific rates were reported and the female rate was positive, was undefined for 29 region-years (23 suppressed, 2 unavailable, 4 with a zero female rate). Suppressed and unavailable values stayed missing, and reported zeros were retained. Four region-years were excluded from summaries, leaving 136: Nunavut 2023 (partial year) and the 2025 values for British Columbia, Quebec, and Canada (reporting changes).

Table 1 reports, for each region and measure, the eligible, numeric, and missing years and the mean (sample SD) of the available annual values; these are arithmetic summaries, not pooled-period rates. Figure 1 shows annual overall rates for each region against Canada, and Figure 2 shows annual male-to-female ratios. Nunavut 2023 is omitted from the figures, and the flagged 2025 values are marked as cautions.

These summaries are descriptive and do not establish causal effects. Rates are crude, not age-adjusted, so differences between regions may partly reflect age structure. Suppression of small counts leaves the sex-specific series for Yukon and the Northwest Territories incomplete (5 and 6 of 10 years) even though their overall series are complete, so their means are not comparable across rows. Nunavut's only full-year reported values are zeros for 2016-2018, with 2019-2022 suppressed, so we do not interpret its mean. Prince Edward Island has no ratio because all its sex-specific values are suppressed; a missing ratio does not imply equal rates.
