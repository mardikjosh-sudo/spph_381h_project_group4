# Initial visual plan

- **Planned M1 figure:** A small-panel line chart of annual reported crude apparent opioid toxicity death rates per 100,000 population, 2016–2025. Each province or territory gets a panel. A muted reference line uses PHAC’s reported **Canada rate**, not an average of provincial rates. Panels share a zero-based vertical scale.

- **Audience purpose:** Help provincial and regional public-health readers see how each jurisdiction’s reported rate changed and where comparison with the Canada rate warrants closer analysis.

- **Missing data and coverage:** Suppressed and unavailable values will appear as gaps, never zero. Nunavut’s 2023 row will be retained in the data table with a partial-year flag but omitted from the full-year plot because data from July onward were unavailable. The 2025 points for British Columbia and Quebec, and the Canada reference, will be separated from their 2024 lines to flag reporting changes. [PHAC technical notes](https://health-infobase.canada.ca/substance-related-harms/opioids-stimulants/technical-notes.html)

- **Main misinterpretation risk:** Differences in crude rates can reflect age structure, small populations, reporting practices, and investigation delays. The caption will identify the source, measure, gaps, preliminary recent data, and definition changes. The title and interpretation will remain descriptive and will not claim that a jurisdiction or policy caused a change.

For the later secondary question, we will make a separate small-panel plot of the **male-to-female crude-rate ratio**. A ratio above 1 means the reported male rate is higher. Prince Edward Island and Nunavut have no defined ratios in this release, so their panels will say so; gaps for other regions will remain visible. Ratios calculated from PHAC’s published rounded rates are approximate. The source’s sex categories should not be described as gender identity.
