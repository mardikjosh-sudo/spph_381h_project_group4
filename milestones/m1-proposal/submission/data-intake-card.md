# Data Intake Card

## Data source and steward

Our source is the Public Health Agency of Canada’s (PHAC) [Age and Sex Distribution of Opioid- and Stimulant-Related Harms in Canada](https://health-infobase.canada.ca/substances/harms/age-sex-distribution-opioids-stimulants/).
PHAC publishes the surveillance results. Hospitalization and emergency department data come from the Canadian Institute for Health Information (CIHI). Mortality and emergency medical services data come from the national harms surveillance system.
Our planned focus is the 2025 age and sex distributions.

## Access method

The report is publicly accessible. The [main surveillance page](https://health-infobase.canada.ca/substance-related-harms/opioids-stimulants/) provides a ZIP data download. We will check that its files contain the age and sex breakdowns needed for our questions.

## File format

The download is a ZIP archive. The formats and relevant filenames inside the archive still need to be confirmed.

## Unit of analysis

Our planned analysis will use an aggregate demographic group for a particular substance, harm type, and reporting period, rather than individual patient records. We will confirm the exact row structure after downloading.

## Key variables

- Age group
- Reported sex
- Substance category: opioids or stimulants
- Harm type: deaths, hospitalizations, emergency department visits, or available EMS responses
- Reporting year
- Reported count or percentage, where available

## Privacy and stewardship

We will use public aggregate results, preserve suppression and missing-value labels, and avoid attempts to identify individuals. We will document the download date and retain source definitions.

## Equity and interpretation

Coverage differs across harm types and jurisdictions. Percentages describe the distribution of recorded harms; they do not directly measure population risk. We will avoid stigmatizing language and will not interpret reported sex as gender identity.

## Indigenous data governance

The blog does not present an Indigenous-identity breakdown. We will check the downloaded data and documentation. If our analysis involves Indigenous identifiers, communities, territories, or services, we will consult the course’s Week 1 guidance before proceeding.
